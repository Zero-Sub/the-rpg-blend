## Today's Coffee

A tasting flight proves more than knowing coffee vocabulary. You have to prepare several drinks correctly, compare them, and explain what you notice. The Module 2 lab does the same thing with database skills: build, query, relate, summarize, change safely, and prove the result.

## Learning Objectives

The learner can independently:

1. Verify the correct IBM i environment and learner schema.
2. Build a small relational schema with appropriate keys and constraints.
3. Load deterministic fictional data and verify row counts.
4. Write SELECT statements with filtering, ordering, expressions, and NULL handling.
5. Join related tables and explain the relationship and expected cardinality.
6. Aggregate data with GROUP BY and HAVING.
7. Perform one controlled data-change cycle using preview and verification queries.
8. Explain the purpose of a view and an index without unsupported claims.
9. Use Bob only for review and troubleshooting while retaining ownership of the solution.
10. Produce evidence another reviewer can use to reproduce the result.

## Why This Matters

A developer is not ready to move into RPG data access merely because individual SQL examples made sense. The next module will combine RPG with database access. Before that, the learner needs to demonstrate that the relational model, target discipline, and SQL safety habits stand on their own.

## Capstone Rules

- Work only in the assigned learner-owned schema/library.
- Use schema-qualified object names.
- Do not copy a complete solution from Bob or another learner.
- Predict important result counts before execution.
- Record actual results and reconcile differences.
- Preview every UPDATE or DELETE target with SELECT first.
- Do not broaden authority to bypass a failure.
- Do not treat a successful statement as sufficient evidence of correctness.

## Capstone

Complete the learner lab in `../lab/module-02-lab.md`.

The lab intentionally gives acceptance criteria rather than a line-by-line copy script. Earlier lessons provided guided SQL. This is the transfer point where the learner must make and explain decisions.

## Bob-Assisted Review

Bob may be used after the learner has a proposed statement or diagnostic to review.

Allowed examples:

- “Review my CREATE TABLE for missing integrity rules.”
- “Explain this SQLSTATE and identify what I should verify.”
- “Review my join for a missing predicate.”
- “Suggest boundary tests for my finished DDL.”

Not allowed for the capstone:

- “Build the entire schema for me.”
- “Write all the required queries.”
- “Give me the answer key.”
- Blindly running SQL generated by Bob.

## Independent Defense

At the end, close Bob and explain:

1. the schema and relationships
2. one constraint and the invalid state it prevents
3. one join and its expected cardinality
4. one grouped query and its result grain
5. the exact safety sequence used for the data change
6. what the view represents
7. what evidence would be required before claiming an index improved performance

The learner should be able to reproduce one SELECT and one JOIN from memory with normal syntax-reference use but without a generated solution.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating the lab as a speed exercise.
- Building in the wrong schema and noticing only after objects exist.
- Using a query that returns the right-looking rows for the wrong reason.
- Adding DISTINCT to hide a join problem.
- Trusting an aggregate without validating the underlying join.
- Changing a permanent seed row instead of using a disposable test row.
- Leaving temporary objects or data behind after the assessment.

## Manager's Perspective

The most valuable observation is not whether the learner remembers every SQL keyword. Watch the behavior around uncertainty. A production-ready associate asks what the target is, checks assumptions, explains the relationship, predicts impact, and stops when evidence conflicts with expectation.

## Knowledge Check

1. Why does the capstone require schema qualification even in a learner-owned environment?
2. What should happen when expected and actual row counts differ?
3. Why is a successful constraint rejection useful evidence?
4. Why must a join be explained in business terms?
5. What is result grain, and why does it matter in aggregation?
6. Why is preview-before-update a stronger habit than simply adding a WHERE clause?
7. What can Bob contribute during the capstone, and what must remain the learner's work?
8. Why is index-performance proof outside the scope of a five-row dataset?

## Completion Criteria

Module 2 is complete for the learner when the lab and practical assessment meet the published rubric, cleanup succeeds, and the learner can explain the work without relying on Bob-generated text.

## Final Sip

The point of the module was never to memorize SQL. It was to become the kind of developer who can ask the database a precise question, make a controlled change, and prove what happened.
